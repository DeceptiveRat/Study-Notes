Great mindset! You **don’t need to be a math expert** to get started with ML, especially for small projects. But understanding some **core math concepts** will help you *deeply* understand what your model is doing.

Here's a **minimal math guide for ML beginners**—*just enough to be useful, not overwhelming*:

---

## ✅ **Math You Should Know (Bare Minimum)**

### 1. **Basic Linear Algebra (5–10%)**

Used in: understanding model internals like weights, layers, and data representation.

* **Vectors and Matrices**: What they are, how to multiply them.
* **Dot Product**: How it's used in neural networks.
* **Matrix Multiplication**: Especially in deep learning.

**Tip**: Learn with small 2×2 or 3×3 matrices and see how they transform data.

📘 Resource: [3Blue1Brown’s Essence of Linear Algebra (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kjBOesZCoqc)

---

### 2. **Basic Statistics & Probability**

Used in: understanding datasets, predictions, evaluating models.

* **Mean, Median, Variance, Standard Deviation**
* **Probability Basics**: Joint, conditional probability
* **Distributions**: Normal distribution (bell curve)
* **Sampling**: Train-test split logic

**Why it matters**: Helps you interpret data and know if your model is doing well or just guessing.

📘 Resource: [Khan Academy Statistics Course](https://www.khanacademy.org/math/statistics-probability)

---

### 3. **Functions & Graphs**

Used in: understanding models like linear regression, activation functions, loss curves.

* **Linear and Quadratic functions**
* **Sigmoid, ReLU** (used in neural nets)
* **Plotting functions** (just so you get what’s happening visually)

📘 Tool: Use [Desmos Graphing Calculator](https://www.desmos.com/calculator) to explore!

---

### 4. **Basic Calculus (Optional for Starters)**

Used in: model training (like backpropagation in deep learning), but **not required to start**.

Just know **what a derivative is** and **how gradient descent works in concept**.

📘 Optional resource: [3Blue1Brown’s Essence of Calculus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WUvTyaaNkzM)

---

## 🧠 Practical Approach:

* ✅ Start coding projects first.
* ✅ Learn math **when you need it** (e.g., “what’s a loss function?” → learn a bit of calculus or stats).
* ✅ Don’t aim for math mastery before coding anything—it’s a trap many fall into.
