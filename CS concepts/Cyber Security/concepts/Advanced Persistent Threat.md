## 1. What is an advanced persistent threat?
in an Advanced Persistent Threat (APT), the intruder establishes a presence in a network to steal data over a prolonged period of time

the goals of APTs fall into 4 general categories:
- Cyber Espionage
- eCrime for financial gain
- Hacktivism
- Destruction

## 2. 3 stages of an APT attack
### (1): infiltration
access is often gained through social engineering techniques
e.g. phishing emails that target high-level individuals

### (2): escalation and lateral movement
after initial access has been gained, attackers move laterally to map the network and gather credentials. They also install backdoors

### (3): exfiltration
attackers typically store stolen data in a secure location in the network until enough has been gathered, after which they exfiltrate it without detection. They may use tactics like DoS attacks to distract the security team while data is being exfiltrated

## 3. Characteristics of an APT attack
symptoms include:
- unusual user activity, such as increase in logins late at night
- widespread presence of backdoor trojans
- unusual data bundles
- unexpected information flows
